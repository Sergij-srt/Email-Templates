The crane lifting a paperclip is deliberately absurd. Yet imagine the same premium model handling both simple request sorting and complex contract exceptions, with nobody asking whether both jobs need it.

My recommendation is to make cost per successful task part of your AI strategy. That connects the technology decision to an outcome finance and the business can actually assess.

Token prices alone will not answer the question. A cheap output that needs repeated correction can become expensive. A premium output can also cost more than the task warrants.

Here is the evaluation I would put in front of the leadership team:

1
 Define success before selecting a model.
Specify the required quality, response time, data protections and escalation rules. Involve the person who will use the answer.
2
 Compare suitable options on the same work.
Test representative requests and difficult exceptions against the same acceptance criteria. Include simpler options where they could meet the requirements.
3
 Count the full operating cost.
Include model usage, retries, human checking, integration and ongoing support. Measure the resources needed to produce an acceptable result.
4
 Route work according to its demands.
Use the simplest option that meets the agreed standard. Send harder cases to a more capable model or a person when appropriate.
 

I recommend starting with one high-volume workflow because repeated small decisions deserve commercial scrutiny. It also gives your team a manageable place to test routing before adding complexity elsewhere.

Keep reviewing the choice as the work changes. A model that suited the pilot may not be the best fit for the eventual volume, risk or quality requirements.

The leadership question is straightforward: what does it cost us to complete this task successfully, including the human effort?
