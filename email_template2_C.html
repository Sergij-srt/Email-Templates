Your most expensive AI model should do the least work

If your CFO discovered that every cloud workload was running on the most expensive compute instance available, it would be stopped immediately.

Yet many companies are building AI exactly that way.

Routine summaries, document extraction, classification, translation, search and agent loops are being pushed through premium frontier models because those models are available, not because those tasks require them.

This becomes dangerous when AI scales. Menlo Ventures estimated enterprise generative AI spend reached $37 billion in 2025, 3.2x the previous year.

Agents make the economics even more important because one business action can trigger dozens or hundreds of model calls.

The board metric should not be cost per million tokens. It should be cost per correct business outcome.

A cheap model that thinks for ten times longer, retries three times and needs human rework can be expensive. An expensive model used only when it materially improves the probability of success can be cheap. That is why model routing needs evaluation data, not ideology.

The architecture I favour is simple.

60% everyday
   
High-volume, low-risk work goes to lower-cost open-weight or smaller models.
30% business-critical
   
Business-critical work goes to the best cost-performance model that meets the control threshold.
10% high-stakes
   
High-stakes work escalates to frontier models.

An illustrative estate may look something like 60% everyday, 30% business-critical and 10% high-stakes. The exact mix is company-specific. The principle is not.

70-90%

For suitable workloads, the cost reduction can move into the 70-90% zone. In August 2026, Reuters reported that one DeepSeek flagship variant was more than 100x cheaper to run on benchmark tests than one Anthropic model.

That does not mean DeepSeek should replace Anthropic. It means frontier-only architecture is becoming economically indefensible.

This is already mainstream. Linux Foundation research reports that 63% of surveyed organisations are using open models, and 89% of AI adopters have at least some open source in their AI infrastructure.

I have reached this view because I am one of very few independent AI people to have had close access across the major US, European and Asian AI labs. I am not paid to defend one model family. I can compare East and West from the enterprise P&L rather than from a vendor roadmap.

Think of model routing as AI FinOps. You would never run every workload on one cloud instance. You should not run every cognitive task on one model either.
