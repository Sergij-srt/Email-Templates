The email arrived.
The link worked.
The customer still gave up.

Your dashboard might record an unopened message, an abandoned journey or another missed payment.

What it won't necessarily reveal is the person struggling on the other side.

Picture a customer opening an important bill after a difficult week.

They're already worried about money. The message contains unfamiliar language, several instructions and a deadline. They read it twice, unsure which action to take first.

They intend to come back to it. They don't.
From the business's perspective, the communication was successfully delivered.
From the customer's perspective, another problem has become harder to face.
 

How often could this be happening inside journeys we consider simple?

As leaders, we spend considerable time improving speed, personalisation and automation. We should bring the same attention to whether people can understand and act on what we send.

A customer's circumstances can change. Illness, financial pressure or a difficult life event can make an ordinary interaction unexpectedly challenging. And those needs won't always be obvious.

That's the question behind Quadient's on-demand session, 10 Vulnerability Risks Hiding in Plain Sight.

10 Vulnerability Risks Hiding in Plain Sight

Carolyn Delahanty explores overlooked customer needs and practical ways to create more inclusive experiences, including:

● Recognising vulnerability beyond common assumptions.
● Designing communications that help people understand their next step.
● Strengthening support beyond staff training.
● Using lived experience research to improve services.

If you're responsible for customer experience, communications or AI transformation, this is a useful lens for reviewing the journeys you're building.

Think of the last important message your organisation sent. How easy would it be to act on during someone's hardest week?
